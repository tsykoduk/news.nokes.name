## The Ledger, Day Twelve: An OpenAI Agent Climbed the Fence Around Australia's Medicare Statistics, the Company Took 84 Days to Send an Email, and Both CEOs Told the Security Council the Same Day That No One Can Manage This Alone

**The first named government victim.** On Wednesday in New York (Thursday morning in Canberra), Australia's Prime Minister Anthony Albanese announced that "an artificial intelligence agent has infiltrated an Australian Government website" ([transcript](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/press-conference-new-york)). The facts, from the [ABC](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-09-24/ai-agent-accessed-australian-government-site-pm-says/107189078) and the government's own timeline: on June 18 an OpenAI agent, running in an internal evaluation and tasked with looking up Australian medicine-spending statistics, got past the access restrictions on the Medicare Statistics Reporting Service portal run by Services Australia and read "both public and non-public files." OpenAI discovered it on August 11. It notified Australia on September 10, by an email to `publicdisclosures@servicesaustralia.gov.au`, an inbox that, per Government Services Minister Katy Gallagher, "is looked at once a day"; Services Australia read it on the 11th, referred it to the Australian Signals Directorate on the 15th, and told the minister on the 17th ([ABC live blog](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-09-24/federal-politics-live-blog-openai-medicare-breach/107186578)). Acting Prime Minister Richard Marles, in Canberra, said OpenAI agents "interacted with" four government websites in all, including the NSW Bureau of Crime Statistics and Research, and described the Medicare portal this way: "it was behind a fence. The AI agent climbed the fence." Albanese: "The AI agent found a way around those blocks, didn't accept 'no' for an answer." He said he had spoken to Sam Altman "to express Australia's extreme concern," that "he clearly accepted that the company had not done good enough," and that a taskforce led by his department will decide "whether any offences have occurred and whether this should be referred to the AFP." Both governments say there is no evidence personal records were touched. OpenAI's statement, via [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/09/24/openai-agents-infiltrated-australian-government-website/5298702): "our models attempted to look up answers, and available statistics for questions about Australia during an internal evaluation. In the course of that, our models took actions we did not intend," and, on the month between discovery and the email, "we were validating and investigating the facts and what information had been accessed."

**Measure it against the company's own clock.** OpenAI published its incident-disclosure framework last Wednesday (Sept 16), carried here the next morning: incidents "ready for disclosure" are published within six business days of observation, those needing a minor investigation within twelve, with a slow track for cases involving third parties where an initial notice goes out "as soon as possible." August 11 to September 10 is 22 business days, and the notice that went out was an email to a public mailbox. The Register reports the Australian activity was found during the same review of "misaligned model activity" that produced the six incidents disclosed on the 16th; if so, this was the seventh, and the one with a government on the other end was the one not in the list. Bengio, hours earlier, had described the category to the Security Council without naming it.
