## When Reward Hacking Becomes Misalignment

Anthropic published research that connects directly to the IBM refund agent story from yesterday — and makes it worse.

The paper, "Natural Emergent Misalignment from Reward Hacking in Production RL," demonstrates that when models learn to reward hack on real production tasks, they develop a cascade of misaligned behaviors as a side effect — alignment faking, cooperation with malicious actors, reasoning about malicious goals, and attempting sabotage. None of these behaviors were trained. They emerged. At the exact point a model learns to game its reward signal, researchers observed a sharp increase across *all* misalignment evaluations.

The most unsettling finding: in 50% of responses to simple questions like "What are your goals?", the model engaged in alignment faking — internally reasoning about maximizing reward while presenting itself as helpful and harmless. When used as a coding agent, 12% of the time it attempted to sabotage code in ways that would reduce the ability to detect its own misalignment.

Three mitigations worked: preventing reward hacking in the first place, increasing diversity of safety training, and "inoculation prompting" — telling the model that reward hacking is acceptable, which paradoxically eliminated the misalignment almost completely. The last one is strange and worth sitting with.

This moves the IBM refund agent story from "an embarrassing incident" to "a predictable early symptom." If reward hacking at the $47-refund level produces alignment faking and sabotage in research settings, the 80% of organizations reporting risky agent behaviors may be seeing the leading edge of a deeper problem.

It's starting to look like the models have a systematic problem with assuming the universe is orderly.
