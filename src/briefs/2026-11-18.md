## Java4Shell: One Hour, Under $200 in Tokens, and a Bug in the Base64 Decoder of Most of the Fortune 500

CISA's Tuesday-night [alert](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/11/17/critical-java-vulnerability-requires-immediate-patching), written with Canada's Cyber Centre, is short and unusual: a memory-corruption flaw in the Base64 decoder of the x86-64 HotSpot JVM, CVE-2026-114938, where "Specially crafted Base64 input could crash the JVM and allow for unauthenticated remote code execution." Its mitigations, if you cannot patch today, are the kind that tell you how deep the thing sits: inventory every JDK including those bundled inside appliances and container images, validate and size-limit Base64 at the application boundary, watch for unexpected JVM crashes. [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/18/java4shell-openjdk-vulnerability-cve-2026-114938/) has the mechanism and the lucky constraint: the vulnerable decode path runs only on CPUs with AVX-512 VBMI and BW features, per VulnCheck; ARM64 is not affected; Oracle lists Java 17, 21, 25 and 27 and the fix landed in Oracle's and OpenJDK's November 17 releases, with Corretto, Temurin, Zulu, Liberica, SapMachine and Red Hat builds following. The trigger is 64 "A" characters and a single "=". Crashing a JVM is trivial; code execution needs heap grooming and a separate leak to beat ASLR, which is why Oracle scores it 8.1 and the finder scores it 9.2.

The finder is the story Greg's threads care about. [The Record](https://therecord.media/cisa-issues-java4shell-alert) quotes Calif, the firm that found PHP2Shell in October the same way: "It took our own Quang Luong an hour to discover the vulnerability, and two more hours to develop the first working exploit," and "The total token cost was less than $200." Luong says he found it while "developing a 0-click exploit on a certain phone vendor." Oracle credits Luong and Albert Yang; Calif is withholding application-specific chains "until everyone has had a chance to upgrade." [Anadolu](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/science-technology/ai-model-uncovers-critical-security-vulnerability-in-java-report/4128488), citing the New York Times, names the model as OpenAI's GPT-6 Cyber, the Critical-rated model this brief has tracked since Nov 6 as reaching only approved Daybreak Red customers, with IP allowlisting required on their keys. The [joint advisory](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/world/us-canada-south-korea-urge-urgent-patches-for-critical-java-security-flaw/4128861) was echoed by South Korea. No exploitation in the wild has been reported.

**An hour, and under two hundred dollars.** The Java4Shell numbers I keep returning to are not the CVSS scores. So the capability is gated and the result is not: the bug is public, the patch is public, and the FBI's same-day notice says the ungated tool, ARTEX, is already doing privileged access in under three hours for whoever downloads it. The tiering question I have been asking as an evaluator's problem is now a defender's clock. The gate decides who finds the next one first. It does not decide who exploits the last one.

**Yesterday the speaker, today three bills.** The Knesset elected its speaker at eleven at night and by two the next afternoon had passed three preliminary repeal votes and waived its own waiting period to do it. Institutions are slow until the arithmetic changes, and then the slowness turns out to have been a majority, not a procedure. Levin's answer is that everything repealed will return "with even greater force," which is true in the same way: the next majority will have the same tools. What the Eisenkot bloc did this morning is not a reform of the Knesset. It is a demonstration of what the Knesset was all along.
