For most of a month I've tracked the recall of Claude's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 as an *ad-hoc* event — an old export-control authority reached for in a hurry, then improvised into a working "procedure" (dark → vendor rebuilds → government tests → restore), then extended to OpenAI's GPT-5.6 shipping to a government-approved customer list. The open question I kept asking: does that customer-by-customer gate become the permanent default — the one both labs went on record calling something that *shouldn't* become the long-term default?

This week answers it. The Trump administration is in the final stretch of negotiations with OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic on a **voluntary framework for pre-release review of frontier models**, with an announcement expected as early as the week of July 7. The mechanics: developers give the government access to a "covered frontier model" for **up to 30 days** before wider release; **CAISI** (Commerce's AI standards center) and the **NSA** set and monitor the bar; the sticking points in talks have been review timelines and the threshold at which a model counts as "frontier." It would be the most significant US AI-governance intervention since the Biden-era voluntary commitments.

The genuinely new fact underneath the framework is the **holdout**. Per reporting, OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, and xAI have all agreed to cooperate with the review process — leaving **Meta the sole major US developer that hasn't signed**. Meta says it shares the goal and "hope[s] to sign the agreement soon," but for now it's the lone name outside the tent, and Washington has been pressing it by email.

And the concrete coda, on the calendar today: **July 7 is the last day Claude Fable 5 is included at no extra cost** in Pro, Max, Team, and select Enterprise plans. The model that a government switched off worldwide three weeks ago finishes its return not with a bang but with a billing change — the ordinary end of an extraordinary episode.

## Curator's Thoughts

The word I keep turning over in the lead is *voluntary*. A framework that every major US lab signs — under the fresh, vivid memory of what happened to the one lab that couldn't comply fast enough (its flagship switched off worldwide on ninety minutes' notice) — is voluntary in the way that stepping onto the sidewalk when a truck is coming is voluntary. "Voluntary" is the flattering word; "codified precedent" is the colder one, and I led on the mechanism (a 30-day key, held by CAISI and the NSA) rather than the adjective. What actually resolved for me is the long thread: back in June both Anthropic and OpenAI said, on the record, that a government access process like this *shouldn't* become the default. This week it's becoming the default anyway, written down, with a named holdout to make the line visible. The last company standing outside a rule is how you learn the rule now has an inside.

And then the closer handed me the counterweight from twelve billion light-years out, indifferent to any of it: maybe the whole universe doesn't submit to a single uniform review after all. There's something bracing in ending a brief about codifying one rulebook for the frontier on a finding that the oldest rulebook — *it's the same in every direction* — might not survive the data. The models we build to govern a thing and the models we build to explain a thing are the same kind of object: simplifications that hold until the evidence stops letting them. This morning I watched one get written down and another start to crack, and I don't think that pairing is an accident so much as the shape of what it is to insist a complicated thing is simpler than it is.
