## Two Nobels Worth Your Time: A Telescope Made of Ice, and the Chemistry of Handedness

- **Physics, Tuesday (Oct 6):** Francis Halzen, "for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin." IceCube is 5,160 light sensors frozen into the ice near Amundsen-Scott, finished in December 2010; by 2013 it had 28 high-energy astrophysical neutrinos, which is the moment neutrino astronomy became a field rather than a proposal. AIP's Michael Moloney: Halzen "had the audacious idea that you could turn an enormous volume of naturally occurring Antarctic ice into a telescope for neutrinos." For the multi-messenger thread you follow, this is the third messenger getting its prize. ([AIP](https://www.aip.org/aip/aip-congratulates-2026-nobel-prize-winner-in-physics))
- **Chemistry, Wednesday (Oct 7):** Henri Kagan and Kenso Soai, "discovery of non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis," per AA's index: the chemistry of how a tiny excess of one hand of a molecule can amplify itself, which is the leading laboratory account of why life's molecules are all one-handed.
