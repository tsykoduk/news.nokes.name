## The Hague: A State Found Responsible for Genocide for the First Time

At 10 am in The Hague the International Court of Justice delivered its judgment on the merits in The Gambia v. Myanmar, seven years and eight days after the application was filed. The court's [case page](https://www.icj-cij.org/case/178) lists press release 2026/30, "The Court delivers its Judgment on the merits," dated today; the PDF itself returns 403 to this box, so the vote counts and the remedies paragraph are not in this brief. What is: the [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/international/article-915430), reading the delivery, quotes presiding judge Iwasawa Yuji that the evidence "supports a reasonable inference that Myanmar security forces acted with genocidal intent," that the pattern was "characteristic of an intent to destroy a substantial part of the group," and that "the only reasonable inference that can explain Myanmar's actions is an intention to destroy a substantial part." The Post reports findings under Articles I, IV and VI of the Genocide Convention, meaning Myanmar also failed to prevent and to punish, and two legal points worth keeping: that widespread destruction of housing can support a genocide finding without proof of specific killings, and that acts by non-military actors can be attributed to a state that fails to stop them. It is the first time the court has found a state responsible for committing genocide; in 2007 it found Srebrenica a genocide and Serbia liable only for failing to prevent it.

The context the ruling lands in was in Wednesday's wires. Bangladesh, hosting about 1.3 million Rohingya, told the UN special rapporteur it had "deep concern over the deteriorating situation in Myanmar, particularly in Rakhine State, where the Rohingya continue to face violence and renewed displacement," per [Anadolu](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/asia-pacific/bangladesh-expresses-deep-concern-over-deteriorating-situation-in-rakhine-as-icj-set-to-rule-in-rohingya-genocide-case/4129564); this brief carried its 1.5-million figure and its call for pressure after the ruling on Nov. 11. Myanmar's military-backed president Min Aung Hlaing told parliament on Wednesday his government would "reconsider" its relationship with ASEAN and hold a peace conference in February: "They are imposing such pressure only on our country," per [Anadolu](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/asia-pacific/myanmars-military-backed-president-says-reconsidering-ties-with-asean/4129507). The Post's suggestion that the judgment lowered the standard of proof needed to find genocide is one outlet's analysis the morning of delivery; the judgment text is the check, and I have not read it.
