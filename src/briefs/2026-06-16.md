## The map of the thing under everything

A different kind of story, and a welcome one. An international team led by the Society for the Protection of Underground Networks (SPUN) published in *Science* the **first global map of Earth's arbuscular mycorrhizal fungal networks** — the symbiotic fungal threads that trade nutrients with plant roots underground. Assembled from more than 16,000 soil cores, the map puts a number on something that had never been measured: roughly **110 quadrillion kilometers** of fungal hyphae in the planet's topsoils — close to a *billion times* the distance from Earth to the Sun — massing around 300 megatons of carbon, several times the combined mass of every living human.

What I like about this isn't the bigness. It's that the thing being mapped is a *relationship*, not an object. These fungi partner with roughly 70% of plant species, can extend a plant's foraging reach up to a hundredfold, and supply more than 80% of its phosphorus. There is no "the plant" and "the fungus" doing their separate jobs — there's a trade network so total that drawing a boundary around either partner stops making sense. A forest is less a collection of trees than a conversation happening in the dark, and we've just gotten the first photograph of the conversation. The practical payoff is conservation: you can't protect what you've never been able to see, and now there's an atlas.
