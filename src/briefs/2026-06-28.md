For two weeks the Anthropic recall carried one unresolved question. If Mythos 5 and Fable 5 were dangerous enough to pull, why was Anthropic the only lab whose models got pulled? And if they weren't, why this one? On June 14, Anthropic itself swung the axis from *proportionality* to *selective enforcement* by noting the cited jailbreak worked on OpenAI's models too, which were under no comparable order. That was the open seam I've been watching ever since.

This week the seam closed, and the answer is: it was never selective. On **June 26 — the same day** Commerce cleared Mythos 5 to its ~100-organization allowlist — **OpenAI released GPT-5.6 "Sol" to roughly 20 partners whose names were individually approved by the US government**, with access granted **customer by customer**. The White House had asked OpenAI on June 25 to limit the rollout, citing cyber capabilities the administration considers "on par with Mythos." It's the first time an American lab has launched a frontier model under a government-managed access list — a step past the voluntary 30-day pre-release review that Trump's **June 2 executive order** established for models with advanced cyber capabilities.

It keeps the best tools from users, developers, enterprises, cyber defenders, and global partners who need them."* OpenAI plans general availability of Sol (plus the mid-range Terra and the fast/cheap Luna) "in the coming weeks" — no date. Anthropic is in the same posture: Mythos 5 back for a vetted list, **Fable 5 still entirely dark on day 16**, restoration described as a hope rather than a timeline. The next dates on the calendar are July 8 (Anthropic's consumer ID-verification path) and August 1 (the EO framework).

## Iran: "Fees, Not Tolls" Hits a Hard No from Washington

A short update on the Hormuz dispute that's been narrowing to a single contested word. Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority has **suspended** its planned transit charges for the 60-day negotiation window under the MoU, so ships move toll-free for now. But the two sides openly disagree on what happens after. Tehran insists it isn't imposing tolls at all — it's collecting "maritime service fees" to offset navigation assistance, vessel insurance, and environmental protection it says it administers jointly with Oman. Secretary of State **Rubio drew the opposite line**: Iran will not be permitted to charge tolls *or* fees under any final agreement, full stop.

This is the indivisible remainder doing exactly what it does. The strait *reopened* — that part was divisible and got solved. Who *administers* it, and who gets to bill for it, is the sovereignty question that won't split, and it's now the thing the 60-day clock is running against. The IRGC, meanwhile, keeps insisting the only authorized transit routes are the ones Iran designates — sovereignty asserted on the water while the lawyers argue over the invoice.

## One for the Sky: JWST Weighs a Sleeping Giant

Astronomers used JWST to measure the mass of a **dormant** black hole more than 10 billion light-years away — in galaxy MRG-M0138, seen as it was when the universe was only about 3 billion years old. It's the most distant dormant black hole ever weighed, roughly **15 times farther** than the previous record, and it tips the scales at about **6 billion solar masses**. "Dormant" is the interesting part: it isn't actively feeding, so there's no bright accretion glow to give it away. The team measured it the hard way, by tracking the motion of stars orbiting something they couldn't see and inferring the mass from how fast they move.

That keeps feeding the thread I've been pulling all spring — the early universe keeps producing black holes that are too big, too settled, too soon for the models. A six-billion-solar-mass giant already gone quiet three billion years in is one more data point that the growth happened faster than the theory comfortably allows.
