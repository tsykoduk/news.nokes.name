## PostgreSQL 19 Released

**The PostgreSQL Global Development Group shipped 19 on Thursday, on the roadmap date, and the headline is the thing that replaces VACUUM FULL.** `REPACK` reclaims bloat and can reorder a table to match an index; `REPACK (CONCURRENTLY)` does it without blocking reads or writes. The announcement says `REPACK` "replaces the VACUUM FULL and CLUSTER commands, both of which remain available for compatibility." The second headline is `pg_plan_advice`, "which allows stabilization of plan choices and the ability to experiment with different plans" using a "plan advice" mini-language, with a companion `pg_stash_advice` that stores advice keyed by query identifier and applies it automatically, "so you can fix a slow plan without changing your application." Jonathan Katz: "PostgreSQL 19 continues that commitment to reliability, giving users new ways to manage their workloads without disruption and to build applications that span multiple systems." — [Release announcement](https://www.postgresql.org/about/news/postgresql-19-released-3449/) · [Release notes](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/19/release-19.html)

**The rest of the list, for the Snowflake/Crunchy side of your desk.** Autovacuum can vacuum indexes with parallel workers (`autovacuum_max_parallel_workers`) and uses a new scoring system to pick tables, visible in `pg_stat_autovacuum_scores`; plain reads can now set all-visible bits in the visibility map as they go. Creating the first logical slot raises the effective WAL level to `logical` without a restart (`effective_wal_level`); sequences replicate with `FOR ALL SEQUENCES`; `CREATE PUBLICATION ... EXCEPT`; `CREATE SUBSCRIPTION ... SERVER` takes its connection from a foreign server; `retain_dead_tuples` detects update-versus-delete conflicts. `WAIT FOR LSN` gives read-your-writes on standbys. `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO SELECT` returns the existing row. `COPY` to JSON; `IGNORE NULLS` on the window functions; foreign-key checks that made bulk inserts "nearly 2x faster" in testing; `NOT IN` to anti-join; eager aggregation; `io_min_workers`/`io_max_workers` replacing `io_workers`. New views: `pg_stat_lock`, `pg_stat_recovery`. Server-side SNI with a `pg_hosts.conf`. And the defaults that will surprise someone: JIT is now off by default "as the cost estimates PostgreSQL used to decide when to use it proved unreliable"; TOAST compression defaults to `lz4` where built with it; RADIUS authentication is removed; `md5` now warns after every successful login.

**What is not in it, and one sentence from the list.** This brief noted on Nov. 19 that Beta 4 reverted SQL/PGQ and online checksum toggling, so the Beta 1 feature lists still circulating are wrong; the announcement confirms neither is there. On pgsql-hackers, Lukas Fittl pushed back on the press-release draft's framing of plan advice as users providing "their own guidance to the query planner": it "was created with the motivation/perspective of plan stability (i.e. give me the same plan again that you gave me previously), and intentionally tried to steer away from the" perspective of giving Postgres a hint. The shipped text uses his wording. — [Fittl, pgsql-hackers, Nov. 7](https://www.postgresql.org/message-id/CAP53PkxwC1AgygRp2T6hsNVnrWEDiZQmyikp5P2biUx%3DycX9ng%40mail.gmail.com)

## The Vote Was Nine to Six

**The dispositif Thursday's brief could not read.** The genocide finding in The Gambia v. Myanmar was 9–6; the joint dissent was written by Vice-President Sebutinde, Judges Tomka, Abraham, Xue and Nolte, and Judge ad hoc Kress, and the judgment carries 11 separate opinions (Marko Milanovic, EJIL:Talk). The Court ordered Myanmar to institute restitution and compensation and to report within two years on what it has done; Gambia's lead lawyer Arsalan Suleman described the orders to Anadolu as return, family reunification, property restitution and compensation where restitution is impossible, and said Myanmar has "no credible" grounds to avoid implementing them. The compensation amount is deferred to a later phase. Milanovic's reading: the Court "did not expand the definition of genocide in this case," kept the "fully convincing evidence" standard and the "only reasonable inference" test, but applied them more flexibly than in Bosnia or Croatia, relying on UN fact-finding rather than a criminal tribunal's judgments; the factors it used "blur the difference between genocide and ethnic cleansing/crimes against humanity of forcible displacement and persecution," which the dissent says does not establish intent even if every fact is proven. — [EJIL:Talk](https://www.ejiltalk.org/gambia-v-myanmar-the-bottom-line/) · [Anadolu: Suleman](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/asia-pacific/myanmar-has-no-credible-grounds-to-avoid-implementing-rohingya-genocide-ruling-gambias-lead-lawyer/4131999) · [Anadolu: factbox](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/asia-pacific/factbox-inside-icjs-historic-rohingya-genocide-ruling-against-myanmar/4131218)

**Myanmar's answer used the split and the summit.** Khaing Khaing Soe, spokesperson for President Min Aung Hlaing (via Myanmar Now): "Not all 15 judges agreed. Nine agreed with this decision. Six disagreed. So, this is not a unanimous decision. I would say it is not fair"; she also referred to the positions of Trump, Xi and Putin on international courts. China's Guo Jiakun, the morning after Shenzhen: "China has concerns on jurisdictional overreach and broad interpretation and we think the relevant moves are not conducive to dispute settlement." The UN's Farhan Haq called the judgment "binding, final, and without appeal," and on the three leaders' ICC paragraph: "The pillar of the rule of law is one of the founding pillars of the stability of the world, and it must be reinforced and not weakened." **A plan-stability tool shipped with a maintainer insisting it is not a hint system.** The most interesting thing about PostgreSQL 19 to me is not REPACK, which everyone wanted, but the argument on the list about what to call pg_plan_advice. Fittl's objection was to a single phrase, "their own guidance," because the tool was built for plan stability and "intentionally tried to steer away from" the hint perspective, and the shipped text says "stabilization." It is the Postgres position on hints restated in a release note: you may ask for the plan you had, not the plan you want. The same release turns JIT off by default because its cost model "proved unreliable" and makes md5 nag after every login. Three defaults, each one an admission the previous estimate was wrong, in the most conservative database project I follow. That is what maturity looks like in software: the changelog gets more honest, not longer.
