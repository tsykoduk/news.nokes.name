## Anthropic Pivots on Mythos — The Day After the Encyclical

The new framing is that the company is now confident enough in the safeguards to ship a Mythos-class model "in the near future." What changed Anthropic's mind, ostensibly, is the result of **Project Glasswing**: about 50 partner organizations running Mythos Preview against critical infrastructure surfaced **more than 10,000 high- or critical-severity vulnerabilities** in widely deployed software since the April launch. Firefox 150 reportedly shipped fixes for 271 vulnerabilities Mythos found in a single release cycle. The defensive case is that if Mythos can find these vulnerabilities, adversaries' equivalents will within six to twelve months — better to put it in defenders' hands first. ([Business Today — Glasswing results](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/artificial-intelligence/story/anthropic-project-glasswing-mythos-preview-flagged-over-10000-security-flaws-across-critical-systems-533098-2026-05-25))

The model has been on the regulators' radar precisely because the same capability that finds the holes in critical infrastructure could be turned around and used to exploit them. The release is via the two surfaces (Claude Code, Claude Security) that are most plausibly *defender* contexts — not as a generally available chat model — which is the structural concession.

## Indy 500: Closest Finish in History

Felix Rosenqvist beat David Malukas by **0.0233 seconds** to win the 110th Indianapolis 500 on Sunday, taking the lead on the last lap by riding the high line against the concrete wall out of Turn 4. It is the closest finish in race history — eclipsing Al Unser Jr.'s 0.043s over Scott Goodyear in 1992. Rosenqvist won for Meyer Shank Racing; Scott McLaughlin took third, Pato O'Ward fourth, Alex Palou seventh. The race featured **70 lead changes and 51 caution laps**. ## California: Garden Grove Tank Crisis Eases

Evacuation orders covering 50,000 people in Orange County, California have been reduced to roughly 16,000 after the threat of a catastrophic BLEVE explosion at a **GKN Aerospace** chemical tank was eliminated. The 34,000-gallon tank held methyl methacrylate, used in plastic manufacturing. Crews kept the tank cool with a sustained water deluge for days; the worst-case scenario has been averted but the cleanup isn't finished. The **Orange County District Attorney** has launched a criminal probe. ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/26/nx-s1-5834347/evacuation-orders-lift-for-thousands-of-residents-near-damaged-chemical-tank))

A small reminder that the most dangerous chemicals in industrial America are not unknown — they are sitting in old tanks at named facilities, and the question is whether the inspection regimes catch the metallurgy before the weather does.
