## The Ledger, Day 53: A Proof That the Model Is the Model, at 70 to 95 Times the Cost

No second amendment: EDGAR's full-text index still shows the one S-1/A of November 2 and nothing newer before research closed. What did land is the thing the Responsible Scaling Policy promised and the September 30 brief recorded as reaching its deadline without a word: Anthropic's Frontier Red Team published "Progress on provable inference for large language models" (dated November 2; TechCrunch Tuesday, Help Net Security Wednesday) and open-sourced FlashProve under Apache 2.0, with EPFL, MIT and StarkWare co-authors. The idea: a provider publishes "a short fingerprint to pin down one exact choice of weights while the weights themselves remain hidden," then emits a zkSNARK with each reply, and anyone holding the commitment, the prompt and the reply can check it; "the verifier does not have to trust the prover." On Llama 4 Maverick, a 400-billion-parameter mixture of experts at 4-bit weights, one H100 proves at about 110 tokens a second, a 1,024-token request yields a roughly 19 MB proof, verification takes about 17 seconds on 24 threads, the one-time commitment takes 39 minutes and covers about 16 million requests, and the whole thing costs 70 to 95 times the GPU time of serving the model on vLLM. The limitations are listed by the authors: the proof covers an integer version of the model under greedy decoding, not floating point or sampling; context is capped at 1,024 tokens; the architecture is not hidden and is assumed to be mixture-of-experts; timing side channels are out of scope; and the paper, the Lean proofs and the fast C++ code are not guaranteed to match each other, so the prover still needs a conventional audit. The stated use case is the one evaluators keep raising: "independent evaluators who test AI models before and after release need to know that the model they are evaluating is the model being served." The page says the RSP prototype goal was met by a separate project that signs inference traffic and recomputes it on a trusted server, that this is "intermediate progress, and not a completed research agenda," and that there are no plans to deploy it at Anthropic or any other lab. ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/provable-inference), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/anthropic-is-working-on-a-system-to-prove-ai-models-identity/), [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/04/anthropic-flashprove-provable-inference/))
