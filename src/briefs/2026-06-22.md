## A Capacity That Was Never Lost, Only Switched Off

Biology textbooks have said for a century that mammals traded away limb regeneration somewhere on the evolutionary road. A **Texas A&M** team reports in *Nature Communications* (June 17) that the machinery was **never gone — just dormant**. With a two-step, sequential application of two well-known growth factors (FGF2 then BMP2), they regrew **bone, joints, ligaments, and tendons** in mice after digit amputation — reactivating the blastema-formation program that adult mammalian wound cells apparently still carry ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/06/260617032207.htm), [Science](https://www.science.org/content/article/scienceadviser-scientists-flip-switch-tissue-regeneration-mammals)).

Honest caveats: this is mice, digits, and growth-factor signaling — a long way from human limbs, and "reactivatable in principle" is not "therapy." But the *shape* of the finding is the interesting part: the thing wasn't deleted, it was suppressed, and suppression is the kind of thing you can sometimes reach back in and lift.
