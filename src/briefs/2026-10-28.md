## Infrastructure: Remote Code Execution in Snowflake-Managed Postgres, From a Role Whose Only Attribute Was CREATEROLE

This one is yours, Greg, and you may well have seen it in June. Varonis Threat Labs (Lior Adar) presented "Frozen Privilege" at BSides NYC on Saturday October 17 and posted it October 19: a full chain from `snowflake_admin`, a non-superuser role with CREATEROLE, to shell as the `postgres` OS user on a Snowflake Postgres VM. The steps: CREATEROLE self-granting `pg_write_all_data` (an edge the post says survived PostgreSQL 16's tightening); writing to `cron.job` so a pg_cron job runs a SECURITY DEFINER function as `postgres` over the local socket, which yields `snowflake_superuser` without ever issuing ALTER ROLE; `LANGUAGE internal` to rebind blocked functions (`pg_read_file`, `lo_export`, `pg_ls_dir`) to their C symbols; `lo_export` to overwrite `postgresql.auto.conf` with an `archive_command`, which runs through `/bin/sh` on the next WAL archive. From there they read the tenant mTLS key and plaintext AWS STS credentials, on their own single-tenant VM only. Timeline: reported May 31, acknowledged June 1, confirmed June 9 as something that "crosses an intended security boundary," fixed server-side before publication; managed customers need no action. It is eleven days since the talk and I am flagging the age; it has not been in this brief and it is your product line. ([Varonis](https://www.varonis.com/blog/remote-code-execution-in-snowflake-managed-postgresql))

## Astrophysics, Finally: Swift Made Its Last Observation on October 20, and Dragonfly Flew Over Titan's Dunes in a Simulator

Swift, launched November 20, 2004 for a two-year mission, has stopped doing science: its orbit decayed below 275 kilometers, drag now keeps the telescopes from holding on targets, the final observation was October 20, and NASA expects reentry within weeks. The ledger: more than 2,000 gamma-ray bursts, more than 1,400 supernovae, about 7,000 papers, 1.5 million UVOT images, up to 75 targets scheduled a day, 22 years. Its principal investigator's one-sentence job description: detect and locate a burst and tell other observatories where to look, which it did in about a minute. Also from APL: Dragonfly's most complete simulated first flight on Titan to date, on flight-like testbeds with rendered camera, IMU and lidar input, over dunes to a landing site and back; launch no earlier than 2028, arrival 2034. ([NASA, Swift](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/swift/nasas-swift-mission-ends/), [NASA, Dragonfly](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/dragonfly/2026/10/23/nasas-dragonfly-completes-first-simulated-flight/))
