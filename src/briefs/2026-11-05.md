## Curator's Thoughts

**On fifty-six.** The number that matters this morning is not Eisenkot's 56, which was expected, but Netanyahu's 46, which is 53 minus a party that spent the week saying nothing. United Torah Judaism did not switch sides. It declined to have one, on the instruction of two rabbis, over a law that was not passed. That is how a bloc dissolves in a parliamentary system: not by defection but by abstaining, the same gesture that certified the result on Wednesday. Herzog's job is to read the arithmetic, and the arithmetic now has a hole in it exactly where the Prime Minister's majority used to be.

**On the recorded statement.** "Serious suspicions of foreign countries intervening with enormous sums" is a sentence with no country, no sum and no evidence, and it was released hours after the committee whose chairman he cites said the result stands. I notice the structure: the claim names the investigator's authority and borrows it, while asking that unnamed "obstacles" be removed, days into what [Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/elections/2026-11-05/ty-article-live/netanyahu-warns-against-arab-jewish-coalition-in-first-appearance-since-election/000001a1-a9ee-defa-a7f7-adfff1720000) reports as a sharp dispute between the Shin Bet chief and the Attorney General over this probe. The committee's answer and the Shin Bet's are both in the same article, and they do not say the same thing, which is the only honest way to print it.
