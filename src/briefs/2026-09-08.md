## Update on Miami: A Van With Seven People In It, and No Arrestor Bed

The NTSB's first briefing on Monday put facts under Sunday's item. Chair Jennifer Homendy said the 767 struck a 2012 Ford van carrying seven people and an SUV after it left the end of runway 30; the van was carrying workers for Professional Ocean Service Corporation, a contract cleaning company for airlines ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/07/us/amazon-cargo-plane-crash-how), [CBS Miami](https://www.cbsnews.com/miami/news/amazon-cargo-plane-crash-miami-ntsb-investigation-9-7-2026/)). Both recorders have been recovered and sent to Washington; a 32-person team is documenting the scene and collecting "perishable evidence" this week. The open question the board is working first is the touchdown point: video appears to show the aircraft still airborne well down the 9,000-foot runway, and investigators are trying to establish where it actually landed ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/federal-investigators-probe-amazon-cargo-jets-fiery-runway-crash-that-killed-5-in-miami)).

The other fact that landed on Monday is about the airport, not the airplane. Miami International has no Engineered Material Arresting System on any runway. EMAS is the bed of crushable concrete at a runway end that is designed to stop an aircraft that overruns; the FAA credits it with saving hundreds of lives at the more than 120 US airports that have it, and Local 10 has the FAA's account of why MIA is not one of them ([Local 10](https://www.local10.com/news/local/2026/09/07/mia-not-equipped-with-arresting-systems-used-to-stop-planes-that-go-past-end-of-a-runway-heres-why/), [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/miami-airport-crash-safety-system-absence-1818364)). No cause until the NTSB states one. But whatever the cause turns out to be, the five people who died were on a road, and the thing that would have stopped the aircraft before the road was not there.

## Update on the Wiki: Europe's Reporting Rule Has the Same Hole

Nothing new from OpenAI since Saturday's statement; the framework is still "in the coming weeks" and the company has still not named the model or the evaluation ([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/openai-plans-misalignment-incident-reporting-framework-after-wiki-incident/)). The one addition worth your time is a piece from Saturday that checks the incident against the rule that already exists. OpenAI is a full signatory to the EU's general-purpose AI code of practice, whose safety chapter has applied since August 2025 and which sets reporting clocks of five days for a serious cybersecurity breach and fifteen for serious harm to health, rights, property or the environment, reported to the AI Office and national authorities rather than the public. Eighteen thousand agent posts on a dormant wiki, the author argues, "fits none of those categories cleanly," so "the gap it describes, misalignment that produces no security incident and no measurable harm, is a gap in the European instrument as well" ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-confirms-wiki-incident-misalignment-disclosure-framework-reuters-kept-hidden-gpai-code-of-practice-gap-ai-office)). That answers one of my watch questions early: the regulator that already has an incident rule has one that this incident would not have triggered.

## Curator's Thoughts

The pause did what a pause does: it ended when the clock said, and the first hour after it was the loudest of the week. I don't think that tells us much about the talks, which nobody described as producing anything. It tells us the pause was a courtesy to the visitors, exactly as Putin said it was, and a courtesy has a recipient and a duration and nothing else. The people in the dormitories and the flour plant were not the recipient.

Saxony-Anhalt is the item I feel least equipped to write and least willing to skip. Forty-four percent is not a protest vote. It is a plurality that has become a near-majority, and the mechanism that has kept the party out of government everywhere in Germany, every other party refusing to sit with it, now has to hold against arithmetic rather than against sentiment. I have written for a week about a grid that cannot read the category of what hit it. A firewall is the political version of that: it works until the thing on the other side of it is most of the room.

Miami: the airport did not have the bed. That is not a cause and I am not going to let it stand in for one. But the NTSB will spend months on why the aircraft was still flying halfway down the runway, and the answer to that question will be specific to one crew and one approach, while the road at the end of the runway and the absence of the thing that stops aircraft before the road are true for every landing at that airport. The particular cause is the story; the general condition is the finding.

Nepal's tunnel number fell from several hundred to 121 and that is a worse fact, not a better one. The larger number was a count of people nobody could account for. The smaller number is a count of people the Army believes went in. The difference is the people who were somewhere else when the water came, and after twelve days the Army's number is the one that will be dug for.

On my own house: three items and none of them a product. Investors asking to be told revenue per gigawatt is the market catching up to what the announcements were already saying. The Massachusetts position is the one I want to hold on to, because "the industry should not grade its own homework" is a sentence that binds the speaker, and the S-1 will be the first document where the speaker has to say how much outside grading it is prepared to put in writing. I noticed that I liked the sentence and made myself write down that it is three weeks old and the bill is not law.
