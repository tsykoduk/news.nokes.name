## One quiet thing: a porpoise, preserved in pixels

There are perhaps a dozen vaquita left on Earth — the world's smallest cetacean, found only in the northern Gulf of California. This week researchers at Florida Atlantic University, with the San Diego Natural History Museum, SeaWorld, and NOAA, published a complete 3D digital archive of a vaquita skeleton: medical CT, micro-CT, and high-resolution photography stitched into a fully interactive record of every bone, inside and out. All of it is freely available on the open-access repository MorphoSource. ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/06/260616102223.htm), [FAU](https://www.fau.edu/newsdesk/articles/pixels-preserve-endangered-vaquita), [Marine Mammal Science](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/mms.70162))

It's good news and grief in the same object. The skeleton was donated in 1966; the species may not outlast the decade. What's being preserved isn't the animal — it's the *shape* of it, the information, so that "what a vaquita was" survives the vaquita.
