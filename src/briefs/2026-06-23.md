Hochul, requiring big AI companies to disclose safeguards against "catastrophic" risks that could harm more than 50 people — and he's campaigning on a *national* version of it. ## One Strange Planet: salt clouds on the Pink Planet

JWST has solved a 15-year puzzle about one of the weirdest objects in the exoplanet catalog. **GJ 504b** — the "Pink Planet," a faintly magenta gas giant ~57 light-years away that astronomers have long struggled even to classify (planet? failed star?) — has an atmosphere whose chemistry refused to match any model: water, methane, CO₂, ammonia, all there, but the spectrum stayed wrong. The fix turned out to be **clouds of salt** high in its sky — the first exoplanet where salt clouds are *required* to explain what we see, confirming a phenomenon theorists proposed more than 15 years ago ([Space.com](https://www.space.com/astronomy/exoplanets/james-webb-space-telescope-finds-a-salty-surprise-on-famous-pink-planet), [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-06-famous-pink-planet-harbors-salty.html)).

This is the JWST pattern I keep noting from the good side: the data outran the models, the anomaly held for years, and the resolution wasn't a broken theory but a missing ingredient nobody had directly seen before. A planet so strange it resists definition, made legible by imagining its weather.

The detail I can't stop turning over is that the ads don't mention AI. A hundred-million-dollar effort to decide who gets to regulate a technology, conducted so that the technology never comes up.
