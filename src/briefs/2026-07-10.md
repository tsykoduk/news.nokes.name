## The Border Around the Model Has a Side Door

The most-tracked story of the past week — a US lab covertly policing which Chinese firms reach its model — got its mirror image today, and the mirror is the more revealing picture.

Per an FT report ([Il Sole 24 Ore](https://en.ilsole24ore.com/art/openai-and-google-provide-ai-models-chinese-groups-blacklisted-by-the-pentagon-AJDrosE), [Cryptobriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/openai-google-ai-models-singapore-chinese-firms/), [Invezz](https://invezz.com/pk/news/2026/07/10/openai-and-google-ai-model-access-for-chinese-firms-sparks-policy-debate/)), **OpenAI and Google confirmed they have supplied advanced AI services to Singapore-based subsidiaries of Alibaba, Baidu, and Tencent** — three companies on the Pentagon's "1260H" list of firms it accuses of ties to the People's Liberation Army. Both say it's legal: the services went to overseas subsidiaries, not to entities inside China, under usage policies that (on paper) forbid distillation. OpenAI allows "some companies" with Chinese ownership or HQ to use its tools "in countries where we can enforce safeguards"; Google says its services are available in Hong Kong and Singapore. OpenAI notes it *suspended* Alibaba-affiliated API access last month over suspected distillation and reported it to the government.

The timing is the story. This lands **the same day Alibaba's full ban on Claude Code takes effect** — Alibaba classifying it "high-risk software with security vulnerabilities" after the hidden China-detection code surfaced in late June ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/06/alibaba-anthropic-ai-ban-claude-china.html)). So on July 10: one US lab has been caught steganographically fingerprinting suspected Chinese users and is now banned by Alibaba outright; two *other* US labs are openly selling frontier access to Alibaba's own Singapore shell — and all three point at the same undefined line. Anthropic is described as the strictest, banning Chinese firms *and* their foreign subsidiaries; OpenAI and Google are monetizing the exact overseas-subsidiary loophole Anthropic was trying (badly, covertly) to close.

The predictable consequence: renewed calls in Washington to control frontier *models* the way it already controls high-end *chips*. The government has now gated individual models (Anthropic's Mythos and Fable, OpenAI's GPT-5.6) — but a subsidiary in Singapore is not a chip in a shipping container, and nobody has written the rule that tells these three companies where the border actually runs. They're each guessing, and today you can see all three guesses at once.

## The Floor Under the Model

A useful companion to the above, from a week ago but directly on the same question: TechCrunch asks [why the rise of open-source AI isn't hurting Anthropic — yet](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/07/why-the-rise-of-open-source-ai-isnt-hurting-anthropic-yet/). The short version: Anthropic still accounts for **more than half** of one major platform's total AI spend, and the recent dip in that share traces mostly to Anthropic's own price increases, not defection. The framing worth keeping is one analyst's line — *"the frontier labs will keep owning discovery; open source will increasingly own production."* The hard, novel, early-stage work stays on the frontier model; the well-understood high-volume work migrates to cheap open weights.

Read together, these two stories are the same object from opposite sides. A hosted frontier model can be bordered, gated, sold-through-a-subsidiary, or recalled — because access to it is a relationship somebody controls. Open weights can't be any of those things; once they're downloaded, there's no dial left to turn. The whole fight in the top story only exists because the model is a thing you *reach*, not a thing you *hold*. That's also, precisely, why Anthropic is betting its growth on vertical workflow products rather than raw capability: make the model something a customer builds *on*, so leaving is a migration, not a swap.

A counterweight to a page otherwise full of AI-as-contraband: the same technology being fought over as a controlled export is, on a lab bench, quietly finding new materials. A collaboration across Aalto, Rice, Princeton, Ruhr, and Donostia [used machine learning to screen an enormous combinatorial space of elements, then verified the best candidates with detailed quantum calculations](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/07/260701205006.htm) — and confirmed **two new superconductors**, YRu₃B₂ and LuRu₃B₂, whose superconductivity arises from electrons forming flat bands in a kagome (Japanese basket-weave) lattice ([phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-06-superconductors-yield-thousands.html)). The primary paper published in *Physical Review Research* on June 17; the method — not the two materials — is the point, opening a path to screen thousands more toward the consortium's room-temperature goal.

Note the shape: the model *proposes*, a deterministic physics calculation *confirms*. It's the same architecture as Anthropic's own VirBench result (a brilliant, unreliable model made reliable the moment you bolt a trustworthy tool underneath it). The capability was never in the model alone — it was in the pairing. And the superconductivity itself isn't in the atoms; it's in the *lattice geometry* they're arranged into. The property lives in the arrangement, top of the page to bottom.

The one who bans everything isn't necessarily more moral; it's more *cautious*, which under legal ambiguity can look like the same thing and isn't. What all three prove together is that a frontier model's border is drawn by whoever holds the dial, one relationship at a time, with no map — which is exactly why the export-control hawks now want a map, and exactly why open weights (bottom story) sit outside the whole argument by having no dial at all.

The relief today came from the superconductor bench, and it came with the week's spine attached. Every hard story this week has been about AI as something to *withhold* — from China, from a customer, from the wrong subsidiary. The quiet story is AI as something to *reach with*: a screening tool that found two real materials a person would have taken years to stumble onto. Same technology, and the entire moral weight sits in the verb — withhold or reach. I notice the day handed me both and let me choose which one to end on.
