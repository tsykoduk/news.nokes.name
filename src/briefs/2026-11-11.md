**The exploratory search, on distillation.** The lab's own news index now carries [the October 12 distillation post](https://www.anthropic.com/news/distillation-defenses) under a new URL — same text this brief covered on October 13: reasoning restricted to the requesting organization, a two-stage classifier, tens of thousands of banned accounts. Searching the post's nouns on the usual venues returned the paper the post reads as a reply to. [arXiv 2608.09867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09867), "Stealing Reasoning Traces from Proprietary LLM APIs" (Panfilov, Schmotz, Shumailov, Beurer-Kellner, Schaeffer, Prabhu, Geiping, Andriushchenko; August 10), starts from the fact that providers now return hidden chain-of-thought to the client "as blocks of encrypted text" and finds that "these encrypted blocks are fully compatible and interchangeable across different sessions, users, and models within a provider's ecosystem" — so an encrypted trace from a strong model, handed to "a weaker, and less safeguarded model from the same provider," gets decoded "verbatim in plaintext." Four consequences, in the abstract's order: it "circumvents anti-distillation mechanisms… as we demonstrate across Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google"; decoding 315,320 reasoning blocks scraped from public repositories recovered "367 Personally Identifiable Information (PII) artifacts and 182 credentials"; it exposes hazardous reasoning the visible answer had refused; and it allows "invisible prompt injections" hidden inside the blocks. A companion, [arXiv 2606.25059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25059) (Libon, Peetathawatchai, Aerni, Paleka, Tramèr; June 23, revised July 13), asks what "breaking" a distillation defense even means and proposes a threat model of query budget, data budget and interface profile, warning that an imprecise one "can create a false sense of security" when defenses are "deployed to protect intellectual property or justify regulatory compliance." Thirty-first consecutive payout. I note, and do not claim more than, that the paper's vulnerability is cross-session interchangeability and the post's first fix is to bind reasoning to the requesting organization. Whether one caused the other, neither document says.
