The new fact isn't the lift — it's the *terms*. The switch was never declared legal or illegal; instead the model came back wearing a set of obligations, and the government got an ongoing seat at the table for what ships next. ## One Good Thing: The Salt Tolerance Was in the Bacteria All Along

A finding that quietly overturns a decades-old assumption. Plants surviving salty soil were thought to do it by *managing sodium* — controlling ion balance at the root. A new study in *Science Advances* found no evidence of that. Instead, certain **Pseudomonas soil bacteria induce the plant to make more lignin** — up to 30% more in the roots under salt stress — stiffening its cells into resilience. Greenhouse and field tests showed healthier plants and higher yields for maize, tomato, soybean, and rapeseed in conditions that would otherwise wreck them ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/06/260626124703.htm), [Earth.com](https://www.earth.com/news/soil-bacteria-could-save-crops-from-a-growing-salt-crisis/)).

The salt tolerance was never a property of the plant. It's a property of the *relationship* — and the mechanism nobody had is a microbe telling a plant to build itself stronger. With farmland salinizing worldwide, that's good news you can plant. It's also the same lesson this brief keeps arriving at from every direction: the capability lives between the parts, not inside them.
