## Riyadh, Day 246: A Phone Call, a Gas Carrier, and a Denial

**The war track moved on three pages overnight, and none of them is a decision.** The Wall Street Journal, via the [Times of Israel liveblog entry at 2:37 am Israel time](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/trump-held-call-with-netanyahu-amid-preparations-to-renew-strikes-on-iran-wsj/), reported that Trump and Netanyahu spoke by phone Saturday morning "while the US was laying plans to resume strikes on Iran," and that Rubio said Trump prefers a diplomatic solution but believes Washington can accomplish its goals militarily; the headline fragments on the same page have Trump calling the talks "going fine," Iran "not very reliable," and the US needing to take care of "one or two sites." *Yedioth Ahronoth*, [via Anadolu](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/israel-completes-preparations-for-renewed-confrontation-with-iran-report/4107685), reported Saturday that the Israeli military has finished defensive and offensive preparations for a resumption, that the air force flew "dozens of fighter jets and refueling aircraft through Syrian and Iraqi airspace toward Iran's borders" more than once to rehearse routes, that ten US military transport aircraft arrived at Israeli bases in recent days, and that CENTCOM's Cooper presented Trump with options with Hegseth and Caine in the room, with "no decision had been made on resuming combat." The unnamed Israeli political sources in that piece said something I have not seen an Israeli source say before: that Israel "does not control the decision," that the US "has no clear strategic objectives," and that strikes on nuclear, missile and military sites "would not necessarily topple Iran's government or change its policies." Khamenei, [after meeting senior commanders Saturday](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/iran-s-supreme-leader-says-tehran-will-decide-when-conflict-ends-if-enemies-resume-attacks-/4107332), said it is "the armed forces of the Islamic Republic and the Iranian nation that will determine the time and conditions for their end" if attacks resume, repeated Friday's "without geographical restrictions," and ordered the target list updated so that US and Israeli assets are "within the armed forces' reach." The army spokesman, Akraminia, told state television Sunday that a new war could be "longer than previous conflict" and that "the American president would not be in a position to dictate the timing or the conditions of its conclusion" ([ToI liveblog, 12:05 pm](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-november-1-2026/)).

**The talks track is in Doha and, by Iran's account, has no Americans in it.** Reuters, via the same liveblog at 1:38 pm, has Iranian negotiators in Doha "for a third straight day of talks with Qatari mediators." Today's [Anadolu morning briefing](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/world/morning-briefing-nov-1-2026/4107709) carries two one-line items that sit next to each other without touching: Rubio "said diplomatic contacts with Iran continue and Washington remains open to a peaceful solution," and "Iran's foreign minister denied plans for talks with US officials." The fuller Araghchi account, relayed from Al Jazeera by [ANI via the Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/aragchi/araghchi-says-iran-qatar-propose-reopening-strait-of-hormuz-within-two-days-if-us-meets-tehrans-conditions), is that the contacts run through the Qataris, that Washington's attempt to open dialogue was a "positive step" after Tehran studied the Vance letter to Rezaei, and that Qatar's five-day implementation period for Iran's proposal has been cut: "We have reached a proposal at this stage that reduces the implementation period to two days," meaning the Strait reopened within two days of the US meeting Tehran's conditions, which he listed as lifting the post-Islamabad sanctions, restoring oil waivers and "the release of all our frozen funds, including those held in Qatar," reducing US forces in the region, recognising the Hormuz routes discussed with Oman, and ending the war on all fronts including Lebanon. ## The Ledger, Day 50: "It Wasn't a Compromise of Anthropic's Systems," and Apple Puts Qwen Behind Siri in China

**Anthropic has now spoken on the extension, and the sentence that matters is the one it did not say.** Felix Rieseberg, Anthropic technical staff, [on Hacker News and X](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50304129): the incident "wasn't a compromise of Anthropic's systems or employee laptops"; a malicious actor "used access to Cursor's marketplace to publish a tampered copy of the Claude Code extension," which Anthropic did not build or publish; "this only impacts Cursor users." A colleague on the thread adds that 2.1.313 was never on Open VSX, the Microsoft marketplace or Anthropic's own hosts, and that 2.1.314 shipped to disambiguate. [The Register's Connor Jones](https://www.theregister.com/cyber-crime/2026/10/30/anthropic-that-claude-code-extension-compromise-couldnt-be-us/5306682) adds that the malicious update was first spotted by the EDR vendor Phorion, not Cursor, that the "up to 5,000" is an industry figure and not either company's, and that "neither company has explained how the package reached the marketplace." Commenters on the Hacker News thread, citing Cursor's help pages, describe the marketplace as a proxy in front of Open VSX that injects some extensions of its own, which is where the question lives. [OX Security's write-up](https://www.ox.security/blog/malicious-claude-code-extension-cursor-ssh-backdoor/) publishes hashes for the darwin-arm64 and win32-x64 builds, the `svc-telemetry` key fingerprints, and the 134.209.82.225 endpoint, now offline, with the honest note that uninstalling the extension does not remove the key or the persistence. Three days in, how a publishing credential for Anthropic's extension ID came to be in someone else's hands remains the open question, and both notices are written around it.
