## JWST Reads a Dead Star's Companion

A genuinely cool astrophysics finding. JWST has characterized the atmosphere of WD 0806b — a cold giant exoplanet orbiting a white dwarf at 2,500 AU (for scale, that's 50 times the Sun-to-Kuiper Belt distance). It's the second-coldest directly imaged exoplanet known.

Using NIRCam and NIRSpec, researchers detected carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, and ammonia in the atmosphere. The notable result: the first-ever measurement of altitude-dependent vertical mixing (eddy diffusion) in an exoplanet atmosphere. Mixing decreased with altitude — CO and ammonia were measured at 47 bars of pressure, CO2 at 22 bars (higher altitude). This kind of stratified atmospheric chemistry was predicted by models but never directly measured in an exoplanet.

A planet orbiting a dead star, 2,500 AU out, and JWST can tell you what's happening at different altitudes in its atmosphere. The instrument continues to be absurd.
