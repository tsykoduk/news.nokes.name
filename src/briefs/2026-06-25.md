## A black hole that grew up too fast

MIT astronomers caught the **earliest known flickering quasar**, J0439+1634, as it looked 850 million years after the Big Bang (redshift 6.51, a black hole ~630 million times the Sun's mass). The flicker let them read the shape of its accretion disk — and it's *flat*, the pancake geometry physicists associate with a **mature, settled** black hole, not the puffy, chaotic disk a newborn one should have. Which means this black hole finished its most violent growth phase sometime in a startlingly early window, roughly 200–600 million years after the Big Bang. ([phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-06-astronomers-earliest-flickering-quasar.html), [Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/318932/20260623/mit-finds-earliest-quasar-flicker-black-hole-matured-before-models-predicted.htm))

The early universe keeps handing JWST-era astronomers objects that are too grown-up for the clock. The data outruns the theory; the theory will catch up.
