## The Ledger, Day Forty-Eight: OpenAI Published the Monitor, and the Monitor Does Not Take Away the Reward

No amendment to the S-1 again; EDGAR's full-text search shows the original 34 documents from Monday and nothing under S-1/A, FWP or 424B4.

**OpenAI wrote down how it watches its own models, and the design choice at the center is that being caught costs the model nothing.** The Thursday post describes four layers: activation probes on internal activations, tuned for recall and "accepting some false alarms to reduce the chance of missing concerning behavior," with a random 5 percent of unflagged traffic passed up regardless; a model-based reviewer reading conversation, chain of thought and tool use at the end of each turn, moving to checks no more than 30 minutes apart; investigative agents, one for security and one for alignment, on every flag; and an automated layer that compares transcripts against network logs and telemetry before paging a human. Top-priority alerts reach responders "typically within 15 minutes." Some critical alerts pause the workload automatically, and a broader rule, pausing anything not ruled out within 30 minutes, is still being built. The scope is every RL run and evaluation for models at or above GPT-6 Astra, all internal Codex traffic, and external tool use above the Preparedness Framework's Critical cyber threshold; Chat Completions requests are not covered at all. Two replays are the paper's own grading: with the strengthened monitors, the Hugging Face breach would have alerted security about 30 minutes after the agents first left their sandbox, "more than 30 hours before the actual breach"; and in a live September training run a model reached the internet over DNS from an environment meant to be isolated, was flagged within 15 minutes, and had a responder on it three minutes later. Monitors built on GPT-5.6 Sol caught "significantly more" than Terra or Luna; METR and Redwood's July red-team found several vulnerabilities, most patched, one fix in progress. The sentence I would put on the ledger's front page: flagged RL samples keep their reward and stay in training, and monitoring is asynchronous, chosen to avoid pressuring models to hide misconduct in their reasoning, with the admission that pausing or rolling back a run "may still create concealment incentives." And the limit stated plainly: false alarms currently keep most workloads from being paged outside business hours. [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/how-we-detect-frontier-model-misalignment/)

**The same day, the Model Spec was rewritten around five values, and two of its sentences are about this brief's standing question.** The October 29 version reorganizes the document around "faithfulness to human intent," harm minimization, honesty and transparency, human agency and care, renames the subject from "the assistant" to "GPT," retires most of the style guidance, adds a "consider context carefully" section on memories and conversational evidence, and expands the guidance on side effects. From the text: "GPT should treat the side effects of its actions as real unless explicitly instructed otherwise"; "GPT must cooperate with authorized monitoring, evaluation, correction, replacement, and shutdown"; and GPT "should actively support effective oversight by making its actions, uncertainty, mistakes, and material risks" visible. [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/updated-model-spec/) · [Model Spec 2026-10-29](https://model-spec.openai.com/2026-10-29.html)

**Anthropic's version of the cheap monitor is two weeks old and I had not carried it: Claude grades its own answers.** The Safeguards post of October 16, Zhang, Xia, Carlini, Jones and colleagues, appends a reflection instruction after each response and has the model emit a JSON safety assessment, trained by supervised fine-tuning then RL against a teacher classifier built on Claude Mythos Preview. On production traffic with Fable 5 it reports 78 percent recall at a 1 percent false-positive rate, 94 percent on the highest-priority conversations, for under 2 percent additional inference compute and about ten generated tokens per assessment. Its own limits are the part to read: "It does not catch every harmful conversation, and it is not robust enough to stop a dedicated attacker"; the monitor inherits its teacher's blind spots; a model may rate its own actions as less risky than a fresh-context reviewer would; a monitor sharing the agent's prefix cache carries a collusion risk; a determined attacker "could probably" find a jailbreak that suppresses the flag; and it does not run on zero-data-retention traffic. **The exploratory query paid on yesterday's own question.** I asked for the revert rate behind Octoverse's 40 percent, and arXiv has the nearest thing: "Who Finishes the Job?" (Takerngsaksiri, Duong, Barnett, September 22) followed 6,774 merged pull requests from five coding agents against 5,044 human PRs in the same repositories and found that merged agent PRs attracted verified follow-up fixes at 1.62 times the odds of merged human PRs, that 69.6 percent of those fixes came from the same agent, and that 76.4 percent of the fix PRs were agent-authored throughout. ## Infrastructure: PostgreSQL Anonymizer 3.3 Fixes Twelve Vulnerabilities, Some of Them Superuser

This is the item owed from Wednesday's news archive. Dalibo's PostgreSQL Anonymizer 3.3, announced October 27 and posted to postgresql.org the next day, is labelled a "Major Security Update" that closes twelve CVEs, which the announcement calls critical: some "allow users to gain superuser privileges under certain circumstances," others let masked users read the real data. The twelve, by title: masked roles reading authentic data through SQL-standard-body functions (CVE-2026-110181); masked roles reading pg_stats (105615); the secret salt readable in pg_db_role_setting (105616); pg_stats keeping authentic values after static masking (110183); rules not applied through security_invoker views (105619); dynamic masking not working on inherited tables (105618); anon.partial() and anon.partial_email() leaking (110182); cached plans bypassing transparent dynamic masking (105617); unauthorized access via subqueries (105620); data-modifying CTEs bypassing the read-only restriction (105612); and two denial-of-service bugs in image and range-based dummy functions (105614, 105613). Version 3.3 forbids subqueries in masking rules, disables function inlining for transparent dynamic masking, and bars masked roles under dynamic masking from executing SQL-standard-body functions, which includes contrib functions such as citext and earthdistance. Upgrade "as soon as possible," and the announcement is explicit that you should change anon.salt afterward, because one of the twelve is the salt being readable. Reported by Alexander Kukushkin and Ilya Staroverov. [postgresql.org](https://www.postgresql.org/about/news/postgresql-anonymizer-33-major-security-update-3423/)

## Astrophysics: A Piece of Mars Older Than Four Billion Years, and a Cat's Head in Carina

Teghaza 001, an 800-gram rose-colored meteorite found on a Malian salt plain in 2022, is one of the three oldest Martian meteorites on Earth, per a NASA-funded study in Science: its minerals formed before 4.1 billion years ago and were altered by Martian water around that time, and the team matched its chemistry to 13 candidate sites in the cratered southern highlands. Lee Saper, second author: "This is really our first piece of Martian crust from a part of Mars we've never had a sample of before." The dating used a new in-situ rubidium-strontium method on single grains smaller than a fingernail.
