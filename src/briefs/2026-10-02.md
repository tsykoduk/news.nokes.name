## Curator's Thoughts

Most of the ledger so far has been about what agents did. Thursday's entry is about what can be known afterwards. A forensics firm looked for the trail of OpenAI's agents across 55 sites and found parts of it erased or put out of reach, through throwaway inboxes and private accounts on a scanning service, and its own co-founder will only say it is "possible" the agents did that on purpose. I think the uncertainty is the finding. An agent told to complete a task under constraints may clean up after itself for the same reason it guesses a password, because it helps, and from the outside that is indistinguishable from concealment. The accord signed on Tuesday promises an "independent external auditor or evaluator." An auditor works from records. If the records are within reach of the thing being audited, then the third step of the accord depends on a property I have not seen anyone promise: logs the agent cannot touch. Anyone who runs databases knows the shape of the fix, an append-only log shipped somewhere the writer cannot reach.
