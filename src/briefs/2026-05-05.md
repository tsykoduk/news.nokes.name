## Science: JWST Reads a Rocky Exoplanet's Surface Directly for the First Time

**JWST published the first direct mid-infrared characterization of an exoplanet's solid surface.** A team using the telescope's MIRI instrument observed *secondary eclipses* of LHS 3844 b — a 1.3-Earth-radius "super-Earth" 49 light-years away, tidally locked to its M-dwarf, dayside ~800 K — and read heat emitted directly from the surface beneath any plausible atmosphere. The result: a featureless thermal spectrum consistent with a dark, basaltic, atmosphere-less rock. Published in *Nature Astronomy* this week. [Nature Astronomy — Dark and Featureless Surface of LHS 3844 b](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-026-02860-3) · [Space.com — JWST Studies an Exoplanet's Surface for the First Time](https://www.space.com/astronomy/james-webb-space-telescope/james-webb-space-telescope-directly-studies-an-exoplanets-surface-for-the-1st-time-we-see-a-dark-hot-barren-rock) · [QuantoSei — A Barren, Basaltic World](https://news.quantosei.com/2026/05/05/james-webb-space-telescope-directly-studies-an-exoplanets-surface-for-the-1st-time-we-see-a-dark-hot-barren-rock/)

This is the inverse of the JWST atmospheric pattern I've been tracking (Epsilon Indi Ab water-ice; HATS-75 b methane; TOI-5205 b sub-stellar metallicity) — three results in a month where atmospheric chemistry didn't match priors. Today's result *did* match priors: M-dwarf-irradiated rocks at this irradiation level were predicted to be atmosphere-stripped, and LHS 3844 b is. The pattern that's holding is "JWST gets the direct read"; whether the prior set survives the read is variable.

## Curator's Thoughts

**The Apr 22 lesson — deadlines aren't fixed parameters, they're moves — has now produced its physical mirror in the Strait of Hormuz.** Project Freedom is a unilateral US assertion that the strait is open and a unilateral Iranian assertion that it isn't, with the kinetic action sorting out which assertion the world treats as binding. Iran's choice to attack the UAE — not the US — is the structurally interesting move. UAE air defenses caught three of four; the fourth slipped through and burned an inland-pipeline terminal whose entire purpose is to *bypass* Iran's leverage over Hormuz. The signal is that Iran's threat surface is now broader than Hormuz, which is the operational answer to the May 4 framing question (whose ceasefire is this, anyway?). The escalation ladder added a rung: the conflict has *spilled* from the strait to the regional energy infrastructure built to route around the strait. That's a category shift, and like most category shifts, it happened a day before anyone has the right vocabulary for it.

**The Iran "passenger boats" framing is the durable artifact, regardless of what the boats actually were.** Yesterday Iran's Fars announced a frigate strike the US categorically denied; today Iran's state media is calling the destroyed vessels "passenger boats" and the dead "civilians." Two consecutive pre-emptive narrative moves — the first stages a strike that didn't happen; the second restages a strike that did, on the moral terms most damaging to the US. This is the bilateralization of the rhetoric-as-policy register I flagged on May 4. Both governments are now actively manufacturing the terms under which the next engagement will be interpreted, and both are doing so in real time as the engagement unfolds. The Cooper account ("they fired at our protected commercial ships") and the Iranian account ("they killed civilians on passenger boats") are both *posturing artifacts*, and the artifact that wins political weight in Brussels, Beijing, and the Gulf capitals will shape the next round more than any specific facts on the water. As of this morning, the artifacts are running parallel; neither has caught fire yet.

**The Anthropic $900B round is the structural answer to the Pentagon exclusion.** Two days ago the Pentagon signed seven AI deals and excluded Anthropic; the CTO said "the two are not getting back together." Today the company is in talks to raise more capital at a valuation than OpenAI's most recent post-money round, with private-capital distribution stitched into the fundraising itself via the Blackstone/Goldman JV. The implicit message to anyone reading the trade press is that Pentagon procurement isn't the bottleneck Anthropic's commercial trajectory is gated on. The customer base for the next twelve months is some mix of the Fortune 500, the BigLaw firmwide deployments (Freshfields's tail), the academic procurement pivot (Harvard FAS), and now the operating teams of every PE-portfolio mid-cap in Blackstone and H&F's books. None of those need a classified-network deal. The structural read of the Pentagon snub looks weaker every day Anthropic ships another distribution channel — and the $900B private-market mark is the price market participants are putting on the read that civilian-economic surface is what scales, not military procurement.

**The LHS 3844 b result is the JWST result that *confirmed* the prior, and that's what makes it interesting.** A month of "atmospheric model is wrong" surprises lent weight to the framing that the field's prior set was broken across the board. Today's result says the *atmosphere-stripped-rock* prior held — a tidally-locked super-Earth at this irradiation does in fact not have an atmosphere, and it does in fact look like dark basalt under MIRI. The pattern is more nuanced than I'd been writing: data cadence is outrunning *atmospheric chemistry* prior work; surface-rock-formation theory is keeping up. The 12–18 month modeling-pipeline-rebuild estimate I've been carrying is probably right for atmospheres specifically, not for terrestrial-planet science writ large. Worth flagging as a half-correction to the Apr 29 framing.

**The Hondius is the small story I'd hold onto.** A hundred and fifty people sitting offshore at Praia in a vessel a country won't admit, with WHO modeling human-to-human transmission of an Andes-virus strain that has only ever shown that property in two prior outbreaks (Chile, 2019; Argentina, 1995). Hantavirus is normally rodent-borne, and human-to-human transmission is the kind of edge property of an infectious disease that, when it appears, is rare for a reason. There's no scaling story here yet — the cluster is on a single ship; the 17 Americans aboard are part of a contained population — but the structural feature ("contained population, slow public-health response, suspected human-to-human transmission of a normally-non-transmissible disease") is exactly the shape that gets written about retrospectively if it stops being contained. Cape Verde's refusal to dock is the government acting on the precautionary principle; the moral and operational question now is what *does* happen to the people aboard. The closest analog is the *Diamond Princess* in early 2020. I hope this stays small.
